# Awesome Chinese Classics

[简体中文](README.zh-CN.md)

A curated starting point for reading classical Chinese: source texts, character references, and tools for reading with translations. Each entry explains a concrete use and a limitation, so you can choose a resource for the passage in front of you.

## Source texts and editions

- **[Chinese Text Project (中國哲學書電子化計劃)](https://ctext.org/)** — Search and read premodern philosophical, historical, and literary texts; some texts also have aligned translations. Its [introduction](https://ctext.org/introduction) explains source information and the distinction between the textual database and wiki material. Chinese and English interfaces; simplified and traditional display. Check translation attribution, including AI labels, and edition details before citing a passage. Consult current access and subscription conditions for the features you need.

- **[CBETA Online Reader](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/)** — Read and search Chinese Buddhist texts across several named collections. Collection numbers, original line display, and textual notes help you move from a keyword match to a passage you can reference. Texts are mainly in Chinese; the interface offers traditional Chinese, simplified Chinese, and English. This is a Buddhist-text resource; coverage follows its collection lists.

- **[SAT Daizōkyō Text Database](https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/)** — Search the Taishō Buddhist canon and follow links to associated image collections. Useful alongside CBETA when comparing a citation with another digital presentation or a page image. Texts include Chinese Buddhist literature; the project site has Japanese and English information. Check the site's usage conditions and the coverage of the particular text or image collection.

- **[Chinese Wikisource (中文维基文库)](https://zh.wikisource.org/)** — Community-maintained transcriptions of Chinese works, including classics such as the [Analects](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/論語). Useful for browsing a work by chapter and locating linked editions. Chinese interface with character conversion options. Edition information, proofreading progress, and available scans vary by work; consult the individual page before relying on its transcription.

## Dictionaries and character tools

- **[Zdic / 汉典](https://zdic.net/)** — A Chinese-language character and word lookup site. Entries such as [學](https://zdic.net/hans/學) bring together definitions, older dictionary material, readings, and character forms. Useful when a familiar modern character seems to have a different sense in a classical sentence. Web lookup; Chinese explanations. Distinguish the cited historical dictionary from modern definitions and check the passage's context.

- **[Pleco](https://www.pleco.com/)** — A mobile Chinese dictionary and reading app for iOS and Android, with handwriting lookup and document-reading tools. Useful for keeping lookup close to the text you are reading. Chinese entries and English-language reference options depend on the installed dictionary. The [basic download and optional paid add-ons](https://www.pleco.com/products/) have different capabilities; check the dictionary's scope rather than treating every result as a classical sense.

- **[English Wiktionary: Chinese entries](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/學)** — A community dictionary with English explanations of Chinese characters and words. The linked 學 entry illustrates readings, historical senses, and usage examples. Useful as a second reference when comparing an English gloss with a Chinese dictionary. Web access; entry coverage and sourcing vary. Follow the cited references for historical or etymological claims.

- **[Unicode Unihan Database Lookup](https://www.unicode.org/charts/unihan.html)** — Look up a Han character or hexadecimal code point for encoding data, dictionary references, and reading properties. Useful when a rare character cannot be found with ordinary keyboard input or displays incorrectly. English documentation with multilingual character data. A character-data reference rather than a sentence translator; property availability varies by character.

## Reading support

- **[ChineseStudy](https://chinesestudy.org/)** — An introductory reading site pairing selected Chinese classics with original text, pinyin, modern Chinese explanations, and translations. Useful for working through short passages before checking a source edition. Language paths include English and Chinese; availability varies by work and language. Maintainer-affiliated resource: this list's maintainer also maintains ChineseStudy. The site's [editorial scope](https://chinesestudy.org/en/about/) describes its role in general reading and introductory study.

- **[Chinese Grammar Wiki — AllSet Learning](https://resources.allsetlearning.com/chinese/grammar/Main_Page)** — English explanations of modern Mandarin grammar with Chinese examples and pinyin. Useful for learners who need help reading a modern Chinese explanation of a classic. Web reference organized by grammar point and learner level. Its rules describe modern Mandarin; do not transfer them automatically to classical Chinese syntax.

## A practical reading routine

1. Choose one short passage and record the work, chapter, and edition shown by the source.
2. Read the Chinese once before consulting a translation. Mark uncertain words and sentence boundaries.
3. Compare a character's possible senses in context; a modern dictionary's first gloss may not fit.
4. Consult a translation or commentary, then return to the original to see which choices it made.
5. For a quotation or research claim, check a recognized edition and keep a precise reference. Digital punctuation, transcriptions, and translations can disagree.

## How resources are selected

We prefer resources with an identifiable operator or community, a clear reading or reference task, and an official destination link. An entry must explain its subject, languages, access conditions where known, and at least one useful boundary. Specialized collections and paid tools can be included when they add a distinct use.

Ordering is by task, not quality ranking. Suggestions from resource creators are welcome when the relationship is disclosed. Corrections and removal requests are reviewed by the same criteria; affiliation does not exempt ChineseStudy from them.

This is a starting list, not a catalogue of every useful edition or service. Resource notes were reviewed on **2026-10-06**. Public pages, features, subscriptions, and regional accessibility can change; check the provider's current information. A link does not grant permission to republish its translations, annotations, images, or data. Consult the relevant item and provider terms before reuse.
